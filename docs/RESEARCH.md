# Research dossier: Athens and the Academy, 367–335 BC

This is the factual basis for the world, characters and story. Each section ends with **→ In the game**, saying how the research is used.
Confidence labels match `HISTORY_NOTES.md`: **[D]** documented in an ancient source, **[T]** tradition, late or disputed source,
**[M]** modern scholarly inference, **[I]** invented for the game.

Ancient texts are paraphrased (translations used: Jones/Ormerod's Pausanias, Perrin's Plutarch, Hicks's and Yonge's Diogenes Laërtius,
Jebb's and Storr's Sophocles, Gwilt's and Morgan's Vitruvius). Only short phrases are quoted.

## 1. Getting there: the Dipylon and the road to the Academy

- **The Dipylon ("double gate")** was built with the Themistoclean wall in 478 BC and completed in the 4th century. It was a *courtyard gate*:
  an outer and an inner gateway separated by an oblong court, with towers at the corners (later rebuilt larger in limestone).
  It is often called the largest gate of the ancient world (about 1,800 m² including the court). **[D/M]** ([Wikipedia: Dipylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipylon), [ancientathens3d](https://ancientathens3d.com/keramikos-tichi/))
- Next to it, to the south, is the **Sacred Gate**. The Sacred Way to Eleusis leaves through it, alongside the **Eridanos** stream. Between the two gates
  stands the **Pompeion**, where the Panathenaic procession was assembled. **[D]** ([Kerameikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerameikos))
- ⚠️ **The fountain house at the Dipylon dates to 307–304 BC, after our period. Leave it out.**
- **The road to the Academy** ran northwest from the Dipylon through the **Demosion Sema**, the state burial ground for war dead and honoured citizens.
  Pausanias (1.29) walked it in the 2nd century AD and lists more than 40 monuments, among them Pericles, Thrasybulus son of Lycus (whom he calls the
  greatest of all famous Athenians), Conon, Chabrias, and the tyrant-slayers Harmodius and Aristogeiton. The war dead were buried by campaign
  in *polyandria*, under **stelai listing the fallen by tribe**. **[D]** ([Panathenaic Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panathenaic_Way),
  [Archaeology mag.](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/athens/1.html))
- **Distance:** Cicero (*De finibus* 5.1) walked from the Dipylon to the Academy and calls it **six stadia** (about 1.1 km). Modern estimates of the
  Academy's centre put it about 1.5 km from the gate. **[D]** ([Thayer/Loeb](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cicero/de_Finibus/5*.html))
- **Private grave monuments of the 4th century** (Kerameikos "Street of Tombs"): family terraces with marble **naiskos stelai**
  (a figure relief in a little temple frame), **marble lekythoi and loutrophoroi**, and tall slender **anthemion stelai** (crowned with a palmette).
  Examples are the **Dexileos stele** (a cavalryman killed near Corinth in 394 BC, a Pentelic marble relief of a rider charging a fallen enemy, about 1.86 m)
  and the **Hegeso stele** (about 410–400 BC, a seated woman taking jewellery from a box held by her maid). **[D]**
  ([Dexileos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grave_Stele_of_Dexileos), [Kerameikos steles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerameikos_steles))

**→ In the game.** The prologue starts in the **Dipylon courtyard**: two gateways, four towers, and the Pompeion glimpsed to the left. The road out is
lined with casualty-list stelai (names in Greek letters, grouped under the ten tribe names), family grave terraces with naiskos stelai, marble lekythoi,
and cypresses. The Dexileos rider relief is a readable "stele" with a lore card. The walk is compressed to about 550 m.

## 2. The Academy precinct

- **Origin:** a sacred grove of the hero **Hekademos/Akademos** northwest of the city. **Hipparchus** (6th century BC) walled it at heavy public expense,
  which gave rise to a proverb ("the little wall of Hipparchus") for costly projects (Suda τ 733). **[D/T]** ([Suda](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/tau/733))
- **Cimon** (about 460s BC) turned it "from a waterless and arid spot into a well-watered grove, with clear running-tracks and shady walks"
  (Plutarch, *Cimon* 13.8). He probably brought the water from the Cephissus. **[D]** ([Plutarch](https://lexundria.com/plut_cim/1.13.8/prr))
- Aristophanes' *Clouds* (423 BC) already describes the Academy's trees: smilax, the white poplar, the **plane whispering to the elm** in spring. **[D]**
- **Altars and monuments (Pausanias 1.30):** before the entrance an **altar of Eros** (dedicated by Charmus, the first Athenian to do so); in the Academy an
  **altar of Prometheus**, from which the **torch race** runs to the city. The rule: whoever's torch goes out loses; if the leader's goes out the second wins;
  if all go out, nobody wins. There are also altars of the **Muses**, of **Hermes**, of **Athena** "within", and of **Heracles**, and an **olive tree said to be the
  second ever to appear** (after Athena's on the Acropolis). Plato's tomb is "not far from the Academy". **[D]** ([Pausanias 1.30](https://fgh.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0525/tlg001/perseus-eng2/1.29.15-1.30.4))
- **The sacred olives (moriai)** belonged to Athena and were protected by law. Uprooting one was a capital-level offence (Lysias 7, *On the Olive Stump*). **[D]**
- **Archaeology:** the excavations (from 1929) found a **4th-century BC square peristyle building** ("Tetragonos Peristylos", about 40 × 40 m), now argued to be
  the gymnasium or palaestra used for teaching; a Geometric "sacred house"; a later gymnasium; and a boundary stone of the Academy.
  The exact site of Plato's own teaching is unknown. **[M]** ([GTP](https://gtp.gr/PlatosAcademy), [BMCR 2013.09.37](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=37799),
  [topoi.org](https://topoi.org/event/32391/), [Caruso](https://repository-edulll.ekt.gr/edulll/retrieve/11356/3546_Ada%20Caruso.pdf))

**→ In the game.** A walled precinct (rough limestone socle, the "wall of Hipparchus") with the **Eros altar** at the gate and the **Prometheus altar** just inside
(where the torch race starts). The **square peristyle gymnasium** is the world origin, about 44 m square. Separate altars stand for the Muses, Hermes, Athena and Heracles.
**Twelve moriai** are spread through the grove, with one ancient tree singled out as "the second olive". Plane-tree walks follow Cimon's stone-lined channels.

## 3. Plato's garden and the Mouseion

- Diogenes Laërtius (3.5 and 3.20): Plato taught "first in the Academy, then in the garden at Colonus". The phrase is contested by editors. Dion is said to
  have bought Plato "the little garden (*kēpidion*) in the Academy". Plutarch (*De exilio*) says the Academy property cost **3,000 drachmas**. **[D/T]**
  ([Academus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academus), [DL 3](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/3/Plato*.html))
- **Plato built a Mouseion** (shrine of the Muses) in the Academy. **Speusippus later set up statues of the Graces in it** (DL 4.1). **[D]**
- An **exedra** (curved stone bench) is the classic teaching seat, and Vitruvius says gymnasium exedrae were where philosophers sat. **[D]**

**→ In the game.** The **Kepidion** is a walled private garden beside the public grove, with fig rows, pomegranates, Plato's small house with a scroll room, and the
**teaching circle**: an exedra-like ring of stone benches under a huge plane tree. The **Mouseion** has an altar and a small shrine among cypresses and herms.
In Act 4, after Plato's death, Speusippus's **statues of the Graces** appear there.

## 4. The gymnasium building (Vitruvius 5.11)

- **The palaestra:** a square or oblong peristyle court. Three sides have single colonnades and the **south side is double** (to keep out wind-driven rain).
  **Exedrae with seats** open off the colonnades "where philosophers and rhetoricians sit and dispute". Along the double south portico, from the middle:
  the **ephebeion** (young men's hall) in the centre; on one side the **korykeion** (punching-bag room), the **konisterion** (dust room) and the cold bath; on the
  other the **elaiothesion** (oil store) and bathing rooms. **[D]** ([Vitruvius 5.11](https://lexundria.com/vitr/5.11/gw))
- **The xystos:** a covered running track (a stadium-length portico for winter training), with open-air tracks (*paradromides*) beside it. **[D]**
- Athletes trained **nude, oiled, and dusted with fine sand**, and scraped themselves with the **strigil** afterwards. Oil was carried in an **aryballos**. **[D]**

**→ In the game.** The gymnasium is a Doric peristyle court of fine sand (the wrestling *skamma*) with exedrae on three sides and the double south portico holding
the ephebeion, dust room, oil room, punching-bag room and a basin bath. A **stadion-long xystos** (covered track) runs beside an open dromos under the planes.
Athletes are shown in short loincloths and at a respectful distance (design note §4).

## 5. Colonus Hippios and the Cephissus

- **Colonus Hippios**, "Hill of Horses", lies about 1 km northeast of the Academy (modern Agia Eleousa). Pausanias 1.30.4 lists an **altar of Poseidon Hippios and
  Athena Hippia**, a **hero-shrine of Peirithous and Theseus, Oedipus and Adrastus**, and a grove of Poseidon (later burned by Antigonus). Sophocles was born here.
  In 411 BC the oligarchs met at its Poseidon sanctuary. **[D]** ([Colonus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonus_(Attica)))
- **Sophocles, *Oedipus at Colonus* 668–719**, the chorus's praise of the place: the **nightingale** singing in green glades under the **wine-dark ivy**, the
  **narcissus** and golden **crocus** fed by dew, the never-failing springs of the **Cephissus** wandering over the plain, the Muses and Aphrodite, and above all
  the **grey-leaved olive** that renews itself and that Zeus of the Moriai and grey-eyed Athena watch over. **[D]**
  ([Perseus/Jebb](https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0011/tlg007/perseus-eng2/652-694), [Storr on Elpenor](https://www.ellopos.net/elpenor/greek-texts/ancient-Greece/sophocles/oedipus-colonus.asp?pg=51))
- The **Cephissus** basin made the Academy the lushest suburb of Athens, with reeds, fords, frogs and herons.

**→ In the game.** Colonus is a low rocky hill northeast of the grove, with an altar and a hero-shrine, ivy and nightingales at dusk, narcissus and crocus in spring.
It is the night sky viewpoint for Eudoxus. The ode is the source of the area's flora and fauna and of a collectible scroll (in paraphrase).

## 6. The Lyceum (Act 5)

- A gymnasium in a sanctuary of **Apollo Lykeios**, a large wooded area **east of the city wall** (not adjacent to it), watered by channels from the **Ilissos and
  Eridanos**, below **Lycabettus**. It was used earlier by sophists, by Socrates (*Euthydemus*), and for **military musters**. **[D]**
- The **1996 excavation at Rigillis Street** found a **palaestra** identified as the Lyceum's. It is not fully published. **[M]**
- Aristotle, a **metic**, could not own land. He taught in the public gymnasium and its **peripatos** (covered walk). The name "Peripatetic" comes either from that
  walk or from his habit of lecturing while walking. **[D/T]**
- **Theophrastus's will** (DL 5.51–57) gives the school's later property: the **garden, the walk (peripatos), and the houses beside the garden**, left to friends
  "who wish to study and philosophise there together"; **the Mouseion**, where the **bust of Aristotle** and the other offerings were to be put back; a small
  **stoa**; and, from the same passage, **tablets (pinakes) with maps of the earth** to be set up in the lower stoa. **[D]**
  ([Theophrastus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theophrastus))
- Plato's *Phaedrus* (230b) places Socrates by the **Ilissos** under a broad **plane tree** with a fragrant **chaste-tree (agnus castus)** in flower, a cool spring,
  a gentle grassy slope, and the **cicadas'** summer song. The spot may be sacred to Achelous and the Nymphs. At 259 comes the myth of the cicadas, once men who forgot to eat for love of song. **[D]**
  ([Phaedrus 230b](https://bingweb.binghamton.edu/~ascholtz/clas381a/plato_phaedrus_text.htm))

**→ In the game.** Act 5's Lyceum is a separate map by the Ilissos: a run-down palaestra, a sanctuary of Apollo Lykeios, plane trees and a chaste-tree by the river.
The player restores the **peripatos**, builds a **Mouseion**, fills the **library** and **collections**, and sets up **map tablets** in the stoa.
All of these are attested for the school (some only from the next generation, noted in HISTORY_NOTES).

## 8. Ideas to dramatise (primary texts)

- **Meno 82b–85b:** Socrates leads a slave boy to double a square of side 2 (area 4). Doubling the side gives area 16; side 3 gives 9; the answer is
  **the square on the diagonal**. The point: recollection, and the boy discovering what he "already knew".
- **Republic 509d–511e (Divided Line):** four segments: *eikasia* (images, shadows and reflections), *pistis* (physical things), *dianoia* (mathematical objects, reasoning from hypotheses),
  *noēsis* (Forms, dialectic). Proportional segments.
- **Republic 514a–517a (Cave):** prisoners chained from childhood see shadows cast by objects carried before a fire. One is freed, pained by the fire, dragged up the steep ascent,
  dazzled by the sun, first sees shadows and reflections, then things, then the sky at night, finally the sun itself. Then he returns and is mocked.
- **Timaeus 53c–56c:** the elements built from two kinds of right triangle (the half-equilateral and the isosceles). **Fire = tetrahedron, air = octahedron, water = icosahedron, earth = cube**.
  The fifth (dodecahedron) is used by "the god" for the whole. That there are only five follows from vertex angles: the face angles at a vertex must sum to less than 360°.
- **Parmenides 132a–b (Third Man):** if large things are large by sharing in the Large, then the Large and the large things together need a further Form, and so on without end.
  (Aristotle names the objection "the third man" in his later works.)
- **Delian problem:** Delos was told by an oracle to **double the altar** (a cube). Eratosthenes' letter says the Delians sent to **the geometers with Plato in the Academy**.
  **Archytas** (half-cylinders), **Eudoxus** (curves) and **Menaechmus** (conic sections) solved it. Plato reportedly scolded them for bringing in mechanics.
  Doubling the edge gives **8×** the volume; the needed edge is ∛2 ≈ 1.26 (two mean proportionals). **[D/T]** ([Doubling the cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubling_the_cube))
- **Eudoxus' spheres:** each planet is carried on nested spheres turning about tilted axes. Two counter-rotating inner spheres trace the **hippopede** (horse-fetter),
  a figure-eight that, carried along the zodiac, gives **retrograde loops**. **[D via Aristotle Metaph. Λ8 and Simplicius]**

## 9. Daily life details

- **Food:** **barley cake (maza)** and porridge, wheat bread for the better-off, **olive oil, wine always mixed with water** in a **krater** (the mixing ratio set by the
  host), figs, olives, cheese, lentils, beans, onions and garlic. Fish and meat were rarer (meat mostly after sacrifices). Breakfast was bread dipped in wine.
  ([History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/what-did-the-ancient-greeks-eat-and-drink/))
- **Clothing:** linen or wool **chiton** (men's knee-length or shorter), **himation** (a heavy rectangular cloak over one or both shoulders; for philosophers often the only garment,
  worn over bare skin), the short **exomis** (fastened on one shoulder) for labour and exercise, the **chlamys** (short cloak of young men, riders and travellers), and the **petasos**
  (broad-brimmed travel hat). Fabrics were **brightly coloured and patterned** (vase painting, paint traces). Cynics wore the coarse folded **tribōn** with a wallet and staff.
  ([Met Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/ancient-greek-dress))
- **Houses:** mudbrick walls on a stone socle, plastered and whitewashed, fired roof tiles (thatch on the poorest), rooms opening on an inner **courtyard** with a
  **pastas** (roofed colonnade), and a men's dining room (**andron**) behind an anteroom, often the only painted room. ([Olynthus](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/olynthos-project/olynthian-houses/))
- **Money:** obol, drachma (6 obols), mina (100 dr), talent (60 minai). A skilled worker earned about 1–2 drachmas a day.
- **Writing:** wax tablets (*deltoi*) with a bronze stylus, papyrus rolls stored in cubbies or baskets, ink of soot and gum, reed pens.
- **Calendar:** lunisolar; the year starts at the new moon after the summer solstice (**Hekatombaion**). The **Panathenaia** with its torch race falls in Hekatombaion.
  There were torch races at the **Promethia** and **Hephaestia** too. ([Attic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attic_calendar))

**→ In the game.** Food items (maza, figs, olives, cheese, mixed wine), coins (obols and drachmas), wax tablets as the journal metaphor, the krater at evening meals.
Seasonal chapters use Attic month names.

## 10. Lesbos (Act 4 interlude)

- After Hermias' court at Assos, Aristotle and **Theophrastus** (a native of Lesbos) worked around **the lagoon of Pyrrha** (today the Gulf of Kalloni) for about two years.
  Much of the marine detail in *Historia Animalium* comes from there: **cuttlefish**, octopus, sea urchins, migrating birds. **[D/M]**
  ([Aristotle's biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle%27s_biology), [The Lagoon review](https://www.rsb.org.uk/biologist-book-reviews/the-lagoon-how-aristotle-invented-science))

**→ In the game.** A small coastal map: a shallow lagoon, salt flats, fishermen's boats. The player observes and records the cuttlefish's colour changes and its ink, and the octopus.

## 11. Architecture reference (for the procedural generators)

- **Doric column:** height about **5.5–6 × the lower diameter** in the 4th century (Aegina, about 480 BC, is about 5.5). **20 flutes** meeting in sharp arrises. **Entasis** begins about
  a third of the way up and is subtle by the 4th century. The capital is a cushion-like **echinus** under a square **abacus**. The entablature is a plain **architrave**, then a
  **frieze of triglyphs and metopes**, with **guttae** under the regulae and mutules, and a **cornice**. ([Classical order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_order), [Vitruvius 4.3](https://lexundria.com/vitr/4.3/mg))
- Gymnasia were built of local **poros limestone**, often stuccoed and painted (**triglyphs dark blue, metope and taenia bands red**). Pentelic marble went on special pieces only.
- **Roofs:** Corinthian-type fired clay pan and cover tiles, with **antefixes** (palmette) along the eaves.

## 12. Flora and fauna (for the vegetation and audio)

From Sophocles, Aristophanes, Plato and Theophrastus: **olive** (grey-leaved, silver beneath), **oriental plane**, **elm**, **white poplar**, **cypress**, **fig**, **pomegranate**, **ivy**,
**myrtle**, **laurel**, **chaste-tree** (Ilissos), **narcissus**, **crocus**, **reeds**, **smilax**, thyme and rosemary on walls, poppies, chamomile, wild anise.
Animals: **nightingale** (dusk at Colonus), **cicadas** (summer days), **crickets**, the **little owl** (*Athene noctua*, Athena's bird), **herons** and **frogs** at the Cephissus,
swallows in spring, lizards on hot walls, goats and donkeys on the roads.
