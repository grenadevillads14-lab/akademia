# History notes

Everything in the game that touches history is listed here under one of three headings. When the game invents something,
it goes under **Invented for the game**. Sources are cited loosely, by ancient author, for the reader's orientation.

## Documented

- Aristotle (born 384 BC, Stagira) came to Athens and joined the Academy at about 17, in 367 BC (Diogenes Laërtius 5.9, from Apollodorus).
- Plato's second voyage to Syracuse falls in about 367/6 BC (*Seventh Letter*).
- Plato died in 348/7 BC. His nephew Speusippus succeeded him as scholarch.
- Aristotle left Athens after Plato's death: Assos (Hermias of Atarneus), then Lesbos (with Theophrastus), then Macedon as tutor
  to Alexander (from about 343 BC).
- In 335/4 BC Aristotle returned and taught at the Lyceum, a public gymnasium sacred to Apollo Lykeios, east of the city.
  As a metic he could not own Athenian land.
- The Academy was a gymnasium in a sacred grove about 1.5 km northwest of the Dipylon Gate, along the road lined with public graves (the Demosion Sema).
- A torch race started from the altar of Prometheus in the Academy (Pausanias 1.30.2).
- The sacred olives (moriai) were state-protected, and damaging them was a serious offence (Lysias 7).
- Axiothea of Phlius and Lasthenia of Mantinea are recorded as women who studied with Plato (Diogenes Laërtius 3.46).

## Tradition or legend

- Eudoxus of Cnidus leading the school while Plato was in Syracuse: a modern inference, widely repeated, from scattered ancient evidence.
- Axiothea "dressed as a man": reported by later sources (Themistius, Diogenes Laërtius).
- "Let no one ignorant of geometry enter" over the Academy door: first attested over 700 years later (6th century AD).
  **In the game it is only a rumor students joke about.**
- Diogenes the Cynic's plucked chicken ("Behold, Plato's man!"): an anecdote in Diogenes Laërtius 6.40.
- The twelve moriai descending from Athena's olive on the Acropolis: myth. The number twelve is traditional.
