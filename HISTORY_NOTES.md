# History notes

Everything in the game that touches history is listed here under one of three headings. When the game invents something, it goes under
**Invented for the game**. The full sourced research, with links, is in [`docs/RESEARCH.md`](docs/RESEARCH.md).

## Documented (ancient source)

- Aristotle (born 384 BC, Stagira) joined the Academy at about 17, in 367 BC (Diogenes Laërtius 5.9). Appearance: a lisp, slender calves, small eyes,
  conspicuous clothes, rings and cropped hair (DL 5.1). Plato: "Xenocrates needs a spur, Aristotle a bridle" (DL 4.6).
- Plato was in Syracuse about 367–365 and again 361–360 (*Seventh Letter*). He died in 348/7. Speusippus, his nephew, succeeded him.
- After Plato's death Aristotle went to Assos (Hermias), then Lesbos with Theophrastus (the lagoon of Pyrrha), then Macedon as Alexander's tutor (from about 343).
  He returned to Athens in 335/4 and taught at the Lyceum, a public gymnasium of Apollo Lykeios east of the city. As a metic he could not own land.
- Hipparchus walled the Academy at great cost (Suda τ 733). Cimon watered it and gave it running tracks and shady walks (Plutarch, *Cimon* 13.8).
- Dipylon to Academy: six stadia (Cicero, *De finibus* 5.1). The road was lined with the state graves of the Demosion Sema (Pausanias 1.29; Thucydides 2.34).
- In or before the Academy (Pausanias 1.30): an altar of Eros at the entrance; an altar of Prometheus, start of the torch race (with its rules about the flame);
  altars of the Muses, Hermes, Athena and Heracles; an olive "said to be the second to appear". Plato's tomb is nearby.
- The sacred olives (moriai) were state-protected (Lysias 7).
- Plato founded a Mouseion in the Academy. Speusippus later placed statues of the Graces in it (DL 4.1).
- Dion bought Plato "the little garden in the Academy" (DL 3.20). The property cost 3,000 drachmas (Plutarch, *De exilio*).
- Axiothea of Phlius and Lasthenia of Mantinea studied with Plato (DL 3.46). Axiothea read the *Republic* first (Themistius, *Or.* 23) and wore men's clothes (Dicaearchus via DL).
- Xenocrates was grave and slow. Plato told him "sacrifice to the Graces" (DL 4.6).
- Eudoxus lodged at the Piraeus and walked to the Academy every day (DL 8.86). He devised homocentric spheres and the hippopede (Aristotle, *Metaph.* Λ8; Simplicius).
- Diogenes the Cynic: the plucked chicken, "Plato's man" (DL 6.40); "I see a table and a cup but no tableness or cupness" (DL 6.53).
- Delian problem: Eratosthenes' letter says the Delians consulted "the geometers with Plato in the Academy". Archytas, Eudoxus and Menaechmus offered solutions.
- Colonus Hippios: altar of Poseidon Hippios and Athena Hippia, hero-shrine of Peirithous, Theseus, Oedipus and Adrastus (Pausanias 1.30.4). Its plants and birds come from Sophocles, *OC* 668–719.
- The Lyceum's later fittings (garden, peripatos, Mouseion with Aristotle's bust, stoa with map tablets) come from Theophrastus's will (DL 5.51–57).
- Vitruvius 5.11 gives the plan of a palaestra: exedrae for philosophers, a double south portico with the ephebeion, korykeion, konisterion, elaiothesion and baths, plus the xystos.

## Tradition or legend (late, disputed or anecdotal)

- **Eudoxus as acting head while Plato was in Syracuse** is a modern inference. No ancient source says it. In the game the characters arrange it among themselves;
  the codex says it is not attested.
- "The Reader" and "the Mind of the school" as Plato's names for Aristotle come from the Neoplatonic *Vita Marciana*, centuries later.
- Axiothea's disguise comes from Dicaearchus (reliable enough). **Lasthenia's disguise is weakly attested, so she is not disguised in the game.**
- Diogenes the Cynic's anecdotes are delightful and probably partly invented in antiquity.
- **"Let no one ignorant of geometry enter"** over the Academy door is first attested in the 6th century AD. **In the game it is only a rumour students joke about.**
- Twelve moriai descending from Athena's olive: myth. The number twelve is traditional.
- The Delian story's link to Plato himself is chronologically shaky (MacTutor), and Plato's "scolding" may be a later moralising addition.
- The exact location of the gymnasium and of Plato's own teaching is unknown. The 4th-century square peristyle is the best candidate (modern inference).

## Deliberately left out (anachronism)

- The fountain house at the Dipylon (built 307–304 BC).
- Epicurus's Garden on the Academy road (founded 306 BC).
- The late-Hellenistic and Roman gymnasium buildings visible at the site today.

## Invented for the game

- The player character and their friendship with Aristotle. The player's home city (Athens, Thebes, Syracuse or Miletus).
- Exact layout and distances of the precinct (compressed: Dipylon to Academy about 550 m instead of about 1.1–1.5 km).
- The waterworks puzzle network (Cimon's channels are documented; their layout is not).
- Named minor students, servants, potters and the gymnasiarch, and all dialogue lines (paraphrasing known positions and anecdotes).
- The number and placement of herms used as fast-travel points.
- Daily schedules of named historical people.
- Specific events and quests (unless listed above).
